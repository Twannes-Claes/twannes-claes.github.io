2D Physics Engine

C++
SDL
The Research This project was for my Bachelor thesis, where I built a custom 2D physics engine from scratch in C++. My research question was how different shapes, circles, boxes, and convex polygons, affect a simulation's accuracy, performance, and determinism. The Approach I implemented collision detection and resolution for every combination of shapes, using a broad phase (bounding circles) to cheaply rule out most collisions before running the more expensive Separating Axis Theorem on polygons. Detected collisions get resolved through positional correction and an impulse response, factoring in mass, elasticity, friction, and inertia. Key Findings A fixed time step made the simulation fully deterministic, with identical results across repeated runs, while a variable time step caused it to diverge within seconds. Adding the broad phase also turned an exponential growth in frame time into a near-linear one as object count increased. What I Built
  • Collision detection for circles, boxes & polygons (SAT)
  • Broad phase / narrow phase optimization
  • Positional correction & impulse-based resolution
  • Euler, Verlet & RK4 integration
  • Fixed time step for deterministic simulation
